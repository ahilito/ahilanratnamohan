Fair Play

football| dance | theatre | activism


Fair Pay is a football-dance-theatre piece which examines exclusion and inclusion through the lens of football.  A group of 14 Brussels female footballers from different echelons of the female football world come together on the stage; social footballers, football-obsessed, footballers who have just begun and a former Red Flame.  One thing that unites them beyond football and gender is that they are all activists.  Women's football, the mere act of a woman kicking a ball, is inherently activist.  

 

The performance does not dwell on the footballers’ stories of hardship, attempting to find a place in a severely male-dominated domain.  The piece uses it as a departure point, preferring to concentrate on an alternate reality; and the future.  Their stories are pinned together by choreographed movements of football, based on footballer-performance maker Ahilan Ratnamohan’s football-dance-theatre practice.  It was through sharing these movements, during the project Football x Fashion (2024), that the group first came together.  Since then they have continued this practice informally through monthly training. Re-interpreted by this group with varying skill levels, but unabounding passion, they provide a chance to re-imagine football as a collective ritual of dance.


We arrive at the story of a football community that exists in the margins, yet one that flourishes in re-imagining the football world; Teams like Black Mamba’s, Les GogStars, BisousBisous and FootSensible;  Ranked via categories such as ‘Magic’ and ‘Gold’ as opposed to titles like ‘Premier’ and ‘Champions’ which are prominent in professional, global football.  This clandestine football world presents an antidote to the hyper-commercialised and capitalised world of male football which dominates the media.  In their existence they re-imagine football through a queer lens.
